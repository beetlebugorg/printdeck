Hardware

The Slate case

A desk slab for the Printdeck, with the Pi 5 on its rear.

On this page
  1. Print the parts
  2. What you need
  3. Build
  4. Connect
  5. To open the case
  6. The design

A compact slab for the Printdeck hardware: Raspberry Pi 5, the Adafruit 7 inch touch display in portrait, the 2.5 inch SSD with its USB adapter, and the 30 mm fan. The slab is 132 mm wide, 221 mm high, and 35 mm deep. It leans back 15 degrees on a gray foot.

  • The slab holds only the display and the drive. The Pi is on the outside of the orange back plate, as a backpack. Every port has free space for a straight plug and the bend of its cable, and every plug ends inside the outline of the slab.
  • The USB ports point up, so you connect them from the top of the case. The USB-C and micro HDMI ports point to the left.
  • No cable shows on the front or the sides. The display cables run inside the chin, out through two notches in the bottom edge of the back plate, and up the rear: the HDMI ribbon at the left, the touch USB at the right. The HDMI cable is a flat ribbon: a round HDMI cable cannot bend in the 10 mm under the display.
  • The drive cable (micro-B SuperSpeed to USB-A) goes through a notch in the top edge of the back plate and down into the USB 3 port. It is short: 15 to 20 cm.
  • The fan is under the Pi, on four 5 mm bosses, with four M3 screws. A heatsink cools the SoC.
  • The back plate slides up into grooves in the slab and the chin. The chin snaps on with two tongues and holds the back plate. An M3 screw through each side wall of the slab holds the chin, and four M3 screws hold the foot to the chin. Screws hold the chin, the foot, the display, the drive, and the Pi, into heat-set inserts. No part needs supports.

printdeck-slate-build.pdf has the parts list, the print settings, and the assembly steps.

Settings

ProfilesOne Cura profile for each part: run python3 cura/make_profiles.py --install with Cura closed, then pick “Printdeck - Slate …”.
MaterialPLA, 2.85 mm, 0.5 mm nozzle
Slab, chin0.3 mm layers, 1.5 mm walls, 1.2 mm top and bottom, 20 % grid infill, skirt
Back plate0.3 mm layers, 40 % grid infill, skirt
Foot0.3 mm layers, 20 % grid infill, skirt
SupportsOff for all parts. Every pocket, hole, and slot has 45° faces. A slice of each part with supports on (60°, everywhere) adds 0 g of support.

Build

Left and right are as seen from the front of the case.

1

Display

slabdisplay4 × insert (4 mm)4 × M3 × 6
  1. Press four 4 mm M3 heat-set inserts into the four bosses in the slab. Use a soldering iron at 220 °C. Stop when the insert is flush: the face is only 3.85 mm away.
  2. Put the slab face down on a soft cloth.
  3. Connect the HDMI ribbon cable and the USB cable to the display.
  4. Put the display into the slab: glass to the front, sockets at the bottom. The cables come out of the open bottom of the slab.
  5. Attach the display with four M3 × 6 screws.
2

Drive

back platedrive4 × M3 × 5
  1. Put the drive on the flat front of the back plate: adapter up, the M3 holes against the plate. Attach it with four M3 × 5 screws from the rear.
  2. Connect the drive cable to the micro-B socket of the drive adapter. Bend the cable to the rear through the notch in the top edge of the plate.
3

Fan and Pi

back plate (step 2)fanPi 5heatsink4 × insert4 × M3 × 164 × M3 × 6
  1. Press four M3 heat-set inserts into the four tall bosses on the rear of the back plate.
  2. Put the fan in between the four corner guides on the rear, with the cable toward the right of the case. The label side goes away from the plate: the fan blows onto the Pi.
  3. Attach the fan with four M3 × 16 screws through its corner holes into the four bosses. Turn each screw until the fan touches the boss. Do not tighten more: the thread is in plastic.
  4. Put the heatsink on the SoC of the Pi.
  5. Put the Pi on the four bosses: the USB ports up, the USB-C and HDMI ports toward the left of the case. Attach it with four M3 × 6 screws through the Pi into the inserts.
  6. Lead the fan cable around the right edge of the Pi, above the end of the GPIO header. Connect it to the fan connector of the Pi.

Note. Left and right are as seen from the front of the case.

4

Back plate into the slab

slab (step 1)back plate (step 3)
  1. Keep the slab face down. Lead the two display cables through the two notches in the bottom edge of the back plate: the USB cable at the left, the ribbon at the right.
  2. Put the edges of the back plate into the grooves of the slab, at the open bottom. Slide the plate up until it stops at the top wall.

Note. The drive is behind the display plugs. If the plate does not slide, look at the plugs first.

5

Chin

chin6 × insert (4 mm)2 × M3 × 6
  1. Press four 4 mm M3 heat-set inserts into the four holes in the bottom of the chin, at the front corners. Stop when the insert is flush.
  2. Press one 4 mm M3 heat-set insert into the hole in the outer face of each tongue. Stop when the insert is flush.
  3. Put the extra length of the display cables into the chin.
  4. Push the chin up onto the slab: its grooves take the bottom of the back plate. Push until the two tongues click.
  5. Put an M3 × 6 socket head screw through the hole in each side wall of the slab, into the insert in the tongue. The head goes into the recess and is flush with the wall.

Note. The chin holds the back plate. The case is now closed.

6

Foot

foot4 × M3 × 8
  1. Put the case on the foot. The foot is 6 mm in from the front and the sides, and it reaches far to the rear.
  2. Align the four holes in the foot with the four inserts in the chin.
  3. Put the four M3 × 8 screws up through the foot, from below. Tighten them until the heads touch the bottom of their pockets. Do not tighten more.

Note. The screw heads are below the bottom face of the foot, so the foot stands flat.

7

Cables on the rear

3 × twist tie
  1. Lead the drive cable over the top of the Pi. Connect it to the blue USB 3 port next to the board, from above.
  2. Lead the HDMI ribbon to the left, then up the left side of the back plate. Tie it at the pair of holes at the left. Connect it to the micro HDMI port next to the USB-C port.
  3. Lead the USB cable to the right, then up the right side of the back plate. Tie it at the two pairs of holes at the right. Bend it over the top and connect it to the black USB 2 port away from the board, from above.
  4. Connect the printer to the black USB 2 port next to the board. The USB ports point up, so you connect them from the top of the case.
  5. For each tie: put it in through one hole and out through the other, around the cable. Twist it closed on the rear.

Note. No cable shows on the front or the sides. On the rear the cables are part of the look: keep them tight, with no loops.

Connect

  1. Connect the USB-C power supply to the Pi. The port is on the left edge of the Pi. Turn the cable to the rear.
  2. Connect the 3D printer to the black USB 2 port next to the board, from above.

Then switch on. The setup opens on the touch screen.

To open the case

  1. Disconnect the cables on the rear. Remove the four screws under the foot, and lift the case off the foot.
  2. Remove the screw from each side wall of the slab.
  3. Push a pin or a paper clip into the small hole in each side wall, just above the chin. Each pin pushes the ridge of one chin tongue out of its groove. Pull the chin down.
  4. Slide the back plate down and out of the grooves. The drive and the Pi come out with it.

The design

The case is a build123d model in slate/. The CAD files, the STL files and the build PDF come from it.

Parts

FileColourPrint
stl/slab.stlNavyFront face down
stl/chin.stlOrangeFront face down
stl/back.stlOrangeFront face (drive side) down
stl/foot.stlGrayFlat, screw pockets down

The STL files are in their print orientation. A slice of each part with supports on (support angle 60 degrees, supports everywhere) adds 0 g of support. make.sh checks this.

A slab printed before the chin screws has no screw holes. Print stl/drill_guide.stl and drill the two holes with it. Then drill each hole out to 6 mm, 3.1 mm deep, for the screw head (see drill_guide.py). A new slab has the holes and the counterbores.

To check the screen opening before a full slab, print stl/fit_display.stl with the slab profile. It is the front skin around the screen, and prints face down. Put the display glass down into its pocket and look at the dark LCD border on each side of the opening.

Use the Cura profiles in cura/profiles/: run python3 cura/make_profiles.py --install with Cura closed.

Files

  • shape.py contains all dimensions. Change a value here, then run make.sh.
  • parts.py makes the printed parts, the hardware, the plug spaces, and the cables in build123d.
  • model.py checks the fit with a true boolean test of every pair (no hardware, plug space, or cable touches a printed part; no two printed parts overlap; no two hardware parts or cables overlap, except the pairs that mate) and writes cad/*.step (each part and the assembly with colours), stl/*.stl, and the meshes for the renders.
  • cura/make_profiles.py writes a Cura profile for each part, slices each part with CuraEngine for the filament values in the PDF, and slices each part again with supports on.
  • lineart/render.mjs renders the line-art images in three.js in headless Chromium, in the style of the beetlebug.org viewer: an orthographic view, a flat fill, and dark edge lines. Each assembly step is an exploded view. The parts of the step are in the accent colour, with a dashed guide line to their position. It writes a light and a dark image of each view to build/img/ (transparent PNG). make_pdf.py writes the PDF with the light images.
  • make_site.py writes the project website (../docs/index.html and ../docs/img/) from the same text as the PDF. The Pages workflow publishes it with the STL files and the PDF.
  • fit_display.py writes stl/fit_display.stl, a fit test for the screen opening. make.sh runs it.
  • drill_guide.py writes stl/drill_guide.stl, a guide to drill the chin screw holes in an older slab. make.sh runs it.
  • docs/ contains the finished-build image of this README.
  • make.sh runs all steps (about 2 minutes). It stops if the fit check, the mesh check, or the support check fails.

Needs

uv (build123d, trimesh), Node.js (three.js and puppeteer-core, from lineart/package.json), ImageMagick 7, Chromium, and CuraEngine from the Cura AppImage in /opt/ultimaker-cura.
