01Connect to Wi-Fi

At the first start, the deck makes its password and opens the setup.

  1. Switch on the Printdeck. The setup opens.
  2. Tap your Wi-Fi network, then type its password.
  3. Wait until the deck is connected, then tap Next.
  4. Write down the address and the password, then tap Finish.

02Choose your printer

A new Printdeck has no printer config. The deck then shows the printer setup.

  1. Connect the printer to the Printdeck with a USB cable, and switch the printer on.
  2. Wait until the deck finds the board. Tap Search again if it does not show.
  3. Tap Choose printer. The printers of the brand of your board are at the top.
  4. Tap your printer, then Use. Klipper restarts with its config.

03Install the Klipper firmware

The board of the printer must run the Klipper firmware of this Printdeck. The deck writes it through the USB cable.

  1. Open Machine, then Maintenance, then tap Firmware. A new board shows Install Klipper firmware on the setup screen.
  2. Tap Write. Do not switch off the printer until the deck shows the result.
  3. Tap OK. Klipper restarts and connects to the board.

04The deck

Printdeck is a touch screen for the LulzBot Mini 2 and the LulzBot TAZ Workhorse. It runs Klipper. It shows the print, and it controls the printer.

When the printer does not print, the deck shows the idle screen. The largest key is the action that you need most: Print a file.

The keys are the keys of a machine. A key with an LED has two states, and its LED is lit while it is on: the page that is open, the material in the printer, Preheat. A key with no LED does an action: an orange ring shows that it took the tap.

The deck shows the display of the printer at the top of each screen. The keys of the pages are at the bottom of the idle screen. Each page has its title and Back at the top, and its actions at the bottom.

05The display of the printer

When the printer has a display, the deck draws it. The display shows the status screen, the same picture as the idle screen of the deck.

Turn or click the knob to open the menu. The menu has the same pages and keys as the touch screen: the knob moves the cursor, and a click taps the key. The touch screen shows the cursor with an orange ring. The top item goes back: Status on the home menu, Back on a page. A long click also goes back. With no turn for 20 seconds, the display shows the status screen again.

While an action runs, the display shows an animation.

06The deck in a browser

Open http://printdeck.local in a browser on the same network, and log in with the password of the deck. The browser shows the same screens as the touch screen, sharp at the pixel density of the phone. Each browser has its own screens, so a phone can show the files while the deck shows the print.

On a phone, use Add to Home Screen: the deck then opens as an app, with no browser bars.

The page http://printdeck.local/system shows the version, the services, the logs and the API key for slicers. It also installs an update: choose the update file, then Install. The deck writes the other system slot and restarts into it. If the new system does not start, the deck goes back to the old one.

The same page makes a backup of your data and restores it. See Back up your data.

07Send a file from the slicer

The deck answers the OctoPrint API that slicers use to send files and to start prints.

In Cura, add the deck with the OctoPrint Connection plugin. The deck asks "Allow Cura to use the Printdeck?". Tap Allow.

In OrcaSlicer or PrusaSlicer, add a printer of the type OctoPrint with the address http://printdeck.local, and the API key of the system page.

The webcam of the OctoPrint API is the camera of the deck (Settings, then Camera): http://printdeck.local/webcam/stream and /webcam/snapshot, with the API key. The parameter w gives smaller pictures (for example w=320), and fps fewer frames a second (for example fps=2). With no camera, the webcam is the screen of the deck.

08Start a print

The files are the G-code files that the slicer sent, or that you uploaded in the browser. The newest file is at the top.

Each file shows what the slicer wrote into it: the print time, the filament and the layer height. Cura, PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio write this data. A thumbnail shows when the slicer adds one.

  1. Tap Print a file on the idle screen.
  2. Tap the file. Check the print time, the filament and the temperatures.
  3. Tap Print, then Print again.

09Watch the print

During a print, the deck shows the screen of the phase of the print. The deck knows the phase from the printer and from the G-code of the file.

  1. Watch the first layer. Use − and + to set the height of the nozzle while it prints.
  2. Tap Save to keep the Z offset for the next prints.
  3. Use Tune to change the speed, the flow or the fan while the print runs.
  4. When the print is done, wait for the bed to cool, then take the part off.

10Print in two colors

The Colors tool of the Cura plugin gives a model the roll of another color, for example raised letters on a plate. The print pauses at the first layer of that model, and the deck asks for its roll.

On Resume the deck draws a short line at the front of the bed, behind the prime line of the start. The ooze of the new filament stays on that line. The nozzle then goes back to the part with the filament pulled back a little, and pushes it forward at the first point of the layer. Each change of a print draws its line 2 mm behind the line before it.

  1. In Cura, put the letters on the part. Select the letters, open the Colors tool, and tap their roll.
  2. Print. At the first layer of the letters, the printer parks and the deck shows Change filament in orange.
  3. Tap Change filament. Unload, load the roll of the letters, and tap Done.
  4. Pull the ooze off the nozzle with tweezers, then tap Resume.

11Look around during a print

The other screens are read only for the printer while it prints: the files, the filament and the rolls, the history. A tap cannot change the print by mistake. The deck goes back to the print at a pause, at a new print, and after 2 minutes with no touch.

Menu on the screens of the print shows the other screens while the print runs. The print bar at the bottom of each screen shows the print. Tap it to go back.

12The iPhone and Apple Watch apps

The Printdeck app for iPhone shows the deck as its touch screen: the display of the printer, the print, the roll and the model. It can pause, resume and cancel a print, and start a file of the deck. The watch app shows the print on a blue dot-matrix display.

The apps talk to the deck on the local network. The watch reaches the deck on Wi-Fi, or through the iPhone when it is near.

  1. Open Printdeck on the iPhone. It finds the deck on the network. Tap Connect.
  2. Tap Allow on the touch screen of the deck. The app gets its own key, and sends it to the watch.
  3. For push messages, open Menu, then Notifications, and tap Pair for notifications. Allow the notifications.
  4. To add the complication, edit the watch face and choose Printdeck.
  5. To add a widget, touch and hold the Home Screen or the Lock Screen, tap Edit, and choose Printdeck.

The deck sends a push message when a print is done, when it fails, at a pause, after the first layer, and when a roll runs short. Each message is encrypted on the deck. Only the paired phone can read it. The watch shows the notifications of the iPhone. Settings, then Phones, on the deck lists the paired phones.

13Change the filament

Set the loaded filament here, so that Preheat uses its temperatures. The guided change heats the nozzle, unloads the old filament and loads the new one.

The deck keeps a list of your rolls of filament. Weigh a new roll with its reel, and the deck subtracts the weight of the empty reel. Each print subtracts the filament that it used from the roll in the printer.

  1. Open Filament, then tap Change filament.
  2. Tap the roll that goes in, or tap New roll and weigh it. The nozzle heats for its material.
  3. Tap Unload, and hold the filament lightly while it comes out.
  4. Push the new filament into the extruder until it stops, then tap Load.
  5. Check that clean plastic of the new color comes out, then tap Done.

14Move the head and heat

  1. Open Toolhead, and tap Home all after each start of the printer.
  2. Hold the joystick to move the head. Use Z for the height.
  3. Tap a temperature on the idle screen to set it with the keypad.

15Level and calibrate

Maintenance lists the calibrations that your printer has. The list comes from the config of the printer. Open it from Machine.

  1. Open Machine, then Maintenance.
  2. Tap Bed tilt or Bed mesh to probe the bed. The deck shows the action until it ends.
  3. Tap Probe Z offset for the paper test. Move the nozzle down until a sheet of paper drags a little, then tap Accept.
  4. Tap PID tune after you change a heater or a fan. A PID tune runs for about 10 minutes.
  5. Tap Save config to write the results. Klipper restarts.
  6. Tap Pressure advance test to print the tower. Then tap Pressure advance, and type the height of its sharpest corners.

16Look after the printer

Machine has the information of the printer, the history of the prints, the macros, the console and the settings.

17Update the Printdeck

The deck checks for a new version once a day. Machine then shows Software update in orange.

Each version has a signature. The deck installs only a version with a valid signature of Printdeck. It writes the other system slot and restarts into it. If the new system does not start, the deck goes back to the old one.

  1. Open Machine, then Software update.
  2. Read the notes of the new version, then tap Install. The deck does not install during a print.
  3. Wait while the deck downloads and writes the update. It restarts into the new version.

18Back up your data

A backup is one file with your data: the rolls, the print history, the settings, the slicer API keys, the paired phones and the Klipper config. It can also have the G-code files. Make a backup before you change the SD card, and keep it on a computer.

A backup password encrypts the file. Without the password, nobody can open the backup, and the Printdeck cannot restore it. The password cannot be recovered, so keep it safe.

The backup does not have the password of the Printdeck or the Wi-Fi network. A restore keeps the password and the Wi-Fi network of the Printdeck that you restore to.

  1. Open http://printdeck.local/system in a browser, and log in with the password of the deck.
  2. Under Backup, type a backup password of 8 characters or more two times. Select Include the G-code files to put them in the backup.
  3. Click Download the backup. The browser saves a file with the name printdeck-backup-NAME-DATE.pdbk.
  4. To restore, open the same page. Under Restore, type the backup password, choose the backup file, then click Restore. Do not restore during a print.
  5. The Printdeck checks the whole file before it changes a file, so a wrong password or a damaged file changes no data. After the restore, Klipper and the Printdeck restart.

19Try the deck with a simulated printer

The deck can run a simulated printer in place of the real printer. Klipper then runs the config of a real printer on a board in the deck. The board heats, homes, probes and prints in real time, but no motor moves. Use it to learn the deck, or to test a file from the slicer. You need no cable and no printer.

The deck keeps your printer.cfg as printer.cfg.real while the simulated printer is in use. Real printer puts it back with no change. An orange band at the top of each screen shows the simulated printer.

  1. Open Machine, then Simulated printer. A new deck with no printer has a Simulated printer key on the setup screen.
  2. Tap the printer to simulate, then Simulate. Klipper restarts with the simulated printer.
  3. Print as on a real printer: send a file from the slicer, or tap Print a file.
  4. To stop the simulation, open Machine, then Simulated printer, and tap Real printer, then Go back. Klipper restarts with your printer.cfg.

The deck does not change the printer during a print. Wait until the print ends.

20When something goes wrong

When Klipper stops with an error, the deck shows all the lines of its message.

  1. Read the message. It often names the setting in printer.cfg.
  2. After a stop or a power cut of the printer, tap Firmware restart.
  3. After a change to printer.cfg, tap Restart Klipper.

21Settings

The file printdeck.txt on the PRINTDECK partition holds the settings. Put the SD card in a computer to change it. The deck reads it at each start, then clears the Wi-Fi password in it.

wifi_ssid, wifi_password, wifi_countryThe Wi-Fi network
hostname, timezoneThe name on the network, and the time zone
display_rotateThe turn of the screen: 90 or 270 turns a wide display to tall, 0 or 180 shows the native shape. Empty is 0 for a tall display (the Touch Display 2) and 270 for a wide display
touch_invert_x, touch_invert_y, touch_swap_xy1 turns an axis of the touch screen
preheat_tool, preheat_bedThe temperatures of Preheat with no filament set
night_from, night_toThe hours of the night colors
printer.cfg (a file)A Klipper config. The deck takes it at the next start.
