01Connect to Wi-Fi

  1. Switch on the Printdeck. The setup opens.
  2. Tap your Wi-Fi network, then type its password.
  3. Wait until the deck is connected, then tap Next.
  4. Write down the address and the password, then tap Finish.

02Choose your printer

A new Printdeck has no printer config, so it shows the printer setup.

  1. Connect the printer to the Printdeck with a USB cable, and switch the printer on.
  2. Wait until the deck finds the board. If the board is missing, tap Search again.
  3. Tap Choose printer. The printers of the brand of your board are at the top.
  4. Tap your printer, then Use. Klipper restarts with its config.

03Install the Klipper firmware

The printer board must run the Klipper firmware of this Printdeck.

  1. Open Machine, then Maintenance, then tap Firmware. A new board shows Install Klipper firmware on the setup screen.
  2. Tap Write. Keep the printer on until the deck shows the result.
  3. Tap OK. Klipper restarts and connects to the board.

04The deck

The deck shows the idle screen when no print runs. A key with an LED is on while its LED is lit. A key with no LED does an action.

The printer display and the key panel are at the left. The open page is at the right, with Back at the top and its actions at the bottom.

05The display of the printer

The deck draws the printer display. The display shows the same status as the deck.

Turn or click the knob to open the menu. The menu has the same pages and keys as the touch screen. A click taps the key. The top item goes back, and a long click also goes back. After 20 seconds with no turn, the display shows the status screen again.

06The deck in a browser

Open http://printdeck.local in a browser on the same network. Log in with the password of the deck. The browser shows the screens of the touch screen. Each browser has its own screens.

On a phone, tap Add to Home Screen. The deck then opens as an app.

The page http://printdeck.local/system shows the version, the logs and the API key for slicers. It also installs an update file, and makes a backup. See Back up your data.

07Send a file from the slicer

The deck uses the OctoPrint API, so slicers can send files and start prints.

Cura: add the deck with the OctoPrint Connection plugin. The deck asks "Allow Cura to use the Printdeck?". Tap Allow.

OrcaSlicer or PrusaSlicer: add a printer of the type OctoPrint, with the address http://printdeck.local and the API key of the system page.

The webcam is the camera with the use Apps: http://printdeck.local/webcam/stream and /webcam/snapshot, with the API key. Add camera=2 for the second camera, w=320 for smaller pictures, or fps=2 for fewer frames. With no camera, the webcam shows the deck screen.

08Start a print

The newest file is at the top. Each file shows the print time, the filament and the layer height from the slicer. Cura, PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio write this data.

  1. Tap Print a file on the idle screen.
  2. Tap the file. Check the print time, the filament and the temperatures.
  3. Tap Print, then Print again.

09Watch the print

The deck shows a screen for each phase of the print.

  1. Watch the first layer. Use − and + to set the nozzle height.
  2. Tap Save to keep the Z offset for the next prints.
  3. Use Tune to change the speed, the flow or the fan.
  4. When the print is done, wait for the bed to cool. Then remove the part.

10Print in two colors

The Colors tool of the Cura plugin gives a model a roll of another color. The print pauses at the first layer of that model.

  1. In Cura, put the letters on the part. Select the letters, open the Colors tool, and tap their roll.
  2. Print. At the first layer of the letters, the printer parks and the deck shows Change filament in orange.
  3. Tap Change filament. Unload, load the roll of the letters, and tap Done.
  4. Pull the ooze off the nozzle with tweezers, then tap Resume.

11Look around during a print

During a print, the other screens are read only. The deck goes back to the print after 2 minutes with no touch.

During a print, Main is the print. The key panel opens the other pages.

12Make a time lapse

The deck camera captures a picture at each layer change. At the end of the print, the deck makes a video.

For each picture, the printer retracts, lifts the nozzle and moves the head to the left side. A bed that moves in Y goes to the middle of the part, so the bed is at the same place in each frame. On the TAZ Workhorse the nozzle goes to Z 10 mm or more, over the bed washers. Each picture adds a few seconds.

  1. Aim the camera: Settings, then Camera.
  2. Open Settings, then Time lapse.
  3. Tap Time lapse to turn it on.
  4. With two cameras, open Settings, then Cameras. Tap a camera, and check Time lapse or Pictures.
  5. Tap First layer for a picture of the first layer, or a picture and a pause. At a pause, check the layer, then tap Resume.
  6. Print. After the first layer, the deck shows its picture.
  7. At the end of the print, tap Time lapse on the done screen. The video plays.
  8. To see it later, open Machine, then Statistics, then History. Tap the print, then Play.
  9. To download the video, open http://printdeck.local/system in a browser. The Time lapse part has the video. Click its name.

Cura, PrusaSlicer and OrcaSlicer write the layer comments that the time lapse needs.

Park for the frames is on by default. Off keeps the toolhead in the pictures, with no stops.

Crop to the part is on by default. With a calibration of the camera (Settings, Camera, Calibrate) and the park on, each frame shows the part and the space around it. With no calibration or no park, the frames show the whole picture.

The video is a .mov file. The deck keeps the last 3 videos. A backup excludes the videos.

13The iPhone and Apple Watch apps

The iPhone app shows the deck and controls the print. The watch app shows the print.

The apps use the local network. The watch connects on Wi-Fi, or through the iPhone.

  1. Open Printdeck on the iPhone. It finds the deck on the network. Tap Connect.
  2. Tap Allow on the deck.
  3. For push messages, open Menu, then Notifications, and tap Pair for notifications. Allow the notifications.
  4. To add the complication, edit the watch face and choose Printdeck.
  5. To add a widget, touch and hold the Home Screen or the Lock Screen, tap Edit, and choose Printdeck.
iPhone appThe deck screen and its keys. With two cameras, tap a camera name on the camera page, or swipe, to switch. The watch shows the same camera. Time lapse lists the videos of the deck: tap a video to play it, share it, or save it to Photos.
Watch appThe progress and the time left. Tap for Pause, Resume or Cancel.
ComplicationsThe progress on the watch face.
Home Screen widgetsThe status of the print.
Lock Screen widgetsThe same as the complications.
Live ActivityThe print on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.
NotificationsPrint done, print failed, paused, first layer done, and a roll that runs short.

The deck sends a push message when a print ends or pauses, after the first layer, and when a roll runs short. Settings, then Phones, lists the paired phones.

14Change the filament

Set the loaded filament here, so Preheat uses its temperatures. The deck keeps a list of your rolls. Each print subtracts its filament from the roll in the printer.

  1. Open Filament, then tap Change filament.
  2. Tap the roll that goes in, or tap New roll and weigh it. The nozzle heats for its material.
  3. Tap Unload, and hold the filament lightly while it comes out.
  4. Push the new filament into the extruder until it stops, then tap Load.
  5. Check that clean plastic of the new color comes out, then tap Done.

15Move the head and heat

  1. Open Toolhead, and tap Home all after each start of the printer.
  2. Hold the joystick to move the head. Use Z for the height.
  3. Tap a temperature on the idle screen to set it with the keypad.

16Level and calibrate

Maintenance lists the calibrations of your printer. Open it from Machine.

  1. Open Machine, then Maintenance.
  2. Tap Bed tilt or Bed mesh to probe the bed. The deck shows the action until it ends.
  3. Tap Probe Z offset for the paper test. Move the nozzle down until a sheet of paper drags a little, then tap Accept.
  4. Tap PID tune after you change a heater or a fan. A PID tune runs for about 10 minutes.
  5. Tap Save config to write the results. Klipper restarts.
  6. Tap Pressure advance test to print the tower. Then tap Pressure advance, and type the height of its sharpest corners.

17Calibrate the camera

A calibration lets the camera views and the time lapse crop follow the part.

The deck prints 5 towers and you tap them on a picture. A bed that moves front to back goes to two places, so the deck also gets the depth.

  1. Open Settings, then Camera, then Calibrate.
  2. Tap With a print. Clear the bed, then tap Print. The towers print in the loaded material.
  3. After the print, leave the towers on the bed and tap Go on. The toolhead moves to the left side, and the bed moves.
  4. For each mark, tap the bottom or the top of the tower, then tap Use mark. Tap Skip for a mark that you cannot see.
  5. The bed moves to the front. Tap the top of each tower again.
  6. Check that the green lines are on the towers, then tap Save. Remove the towers.

With the nozzle, the nozzle goes to 9 points and you tap the tip. Use it when the camera shows the tip.

Calibrate again after you move the camera or change the size of its picture.

18Look after the printer

Machine has the information of the printer, the history of the prints, the macros, the console and the settings.

19Update the Printdeck

The deck checks for a new version once a day. Machine then shows Software update in orange.

The deck installs only a signed version. If the new system fails to start, the deck goes back to the old one.

  1. Open Machine, then Software update.
  2. Read the notes of the new version, then tap Install. The deck installs only when no print runs.
  3. Wait for the download. The deck restarts into the new version.

20Back up your data

A backup is one file with your rolls, history, settings, API keys, paired phones and Klipper config. Make a backup before you change the SD card.

Keep the backup password safe. Without it, no one can restore the backup.

The backup excludes the deck password and the Wi-Fi network.

  1. Open http://printdeck.local/system in a browser, and log in with the password of the deck.
  2. Under Backup, type a backup password of 8 characters or more, two times. To add the G-code files, select Include the G-code files.
  3. Click Download the backup. The browser saves a file with the name printdeck-backup-NAME-DATE.pdbk.
  4. To restore, open the same page when no print runs. Under Restore, type the backup password, choose the file, then click Restore.
  5. Klipper and the Printdeck restart. A wrong password or a damaged file changes no data.

21Try the deck with a simulated printer

The simulated printer heats, homes, probes and prints in real time, and no motor moves. Use it to learn the deck or to test a file.

Your printer.cfg stays as printer.cfg.real until you tap Real printer.

  1. Open Machine, then Simulated printer. The setup screen also has this key.
  2. Tap the printer to simulate, then Simulate. Klipper restarts with the simulated printer.
  3. Print as on a real printer: send a file from the slicer, or tap Print a file.
  4. To stop the simulation, open Machine, then Simulated printer, and tap Real printer, then Go back. Klipper restarts with your printer.cfg.

The deck changes the printer only when no print runs.

22When something goes wrong

When Klipper stops with an error, the deck shows the message.

  1. Read the message. It often names the setting in printer.cfg.
  2. After a stop or a power cut of the printer, tap Firmware restart.
  3. After a change to printer.cfg, tap Restart Klipper.

23Settings

The file printdeck.txt on the PRINTDECK partition holds the settings. Put the SD card in a computer to change it. The deck reads it at each start, and clears the Wi-Fi password in it.

wifi_ssid, wifi_password, wifi_countryThe Wi-Fi network
hostname, timezoneThe name on the network, and the time zone
display_rotateThe screen turn: 90 or 270 turns a wide display to tall, 0 or 180 keeps the native shape. Empty is 0 for a tall display (the Touch Display 2) and 270 for a wide display
touch_invert_x, touch_invert_y, touch_swap_xy1 turns an axis of the touch screen
preheat_tool, preheat_bedThe temperatures of Preheat with no filament set
night_from, night_toThe hours of the night colors
printer.cfg (a file)A Klipper config. The deck imports it at the next start.
