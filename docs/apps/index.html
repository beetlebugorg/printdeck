The iPhone and Apple Watch apps

The deck on the iPhone and the watch: the apps, the complications, the widgets, the Live Activity and the push messages.

An iPhone app and a watch app for the Printdeck. They read the OctoPrint-compatible API of the deck over the local network.

  • The iPhone app has the look of the touch screen of the deck, in its tall layout: the display of the printer at the top, then the keys and the plates of the print. During a print it has Pause, Cancel and Menu, the layer, the roll (color, a meter of what is left, the name and the grams), and the model as the deck draws it, with the file, the progress and a playhead. A paused print has Resume. With no print, Print a file opens the files of the deck, newest first. Menu opens the setup. At the start, the app shows the start screen of the deck in dots until the first reading of the deck. When the deck has a camera on, its stream is under the display, 5 pictures a second while the app is open. A tap opens it over the whole screen, at 10 pictures a second. That page turns with the iPhone, and a pinch or a double tap zooms.
  • The watch app is a blue dot-matrix display, in the fonts and the icons of the printer display of the deck, over the whole watch. The first page is the progress of the print: the state, the percent in a large font, the bar, the layer, the time left and the end. Under it, in one scroll that the Digital Crown moves, are the picture of the camera of the deck (when it has one, a new picture every 3 seconds while it is on the screen), the model of the print as the deck draws it, the layers and the times of the print, and the filament. During a print, a tap opens a menu with Pause or Resume and Cancel, each with a question first; the crown moves the selection, as the knob of the printer, and a tap selects.
  • The complications are dot-matrix displays too. The rectangle has the percent in the large font, the time left, the bar, and the end and the layer. The circle is a ring of dots that light with the progress, around the percent; with no print, the hotend. The corner and the inline complications are text. On a face with tint the lit dots take the tint; on a face in full color the display has its blue light. Shared/ComplicationLCD.swift draws them.
  • The iPhone has widgets in the same dots. On the Home Screen, the small widget is the status screen of the watch, and the medium widget is the 128 x 64 status screen of the deck. On the Lock Screen are the rectangle, the circle and the inline text of the complications.
  • During a print, a Live Activity shows the print on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island: the model of the print in dots (the hotend before the deck has the model), the time left, the bar, the end and the layer. The app starts it when it sees a print, updates it from its readings, and ends it after the print. Between the readings, the system timers move the time left and the bar to the end time.
  • The deck sends push messages to the iPhone: print done, print failed, paused, first layer done, and a roll that runs short. The messages are encrypted from the deck to the iPhone. When the iPhone reaches the deck and the deck has a camera on, a notification has a picture of the camera. The watch shows the notifications of the iPhone.

The apps read the deck every 5 seconds while they are open. The complication asks watchOS for a refresh every 15 minutes during a print, and watchOS chooses when it runs. Between two reads, its progress goes up in a straight line to the end time, and the time left counts down.

A deck with the lcd field in /api/plugin/printdeck gives the status screen as its touch screen draws it, with the fan and the message. With an older deck, the apps make the screen from the other fields, and the fan shows "--". The model of the print comes from model.png of the deck: in color on the iPhone, in dots on the watch and in the Live Activity.

Folders

  • Shared/: for the iPhone, the watch and the complication.
    • Deck.swift: the API client and the models. DeckModel.swift: the state of the apps.
    • LCDScreen.swift: the display of the printer, from deck/internal/lcd: the status screen, the menus and rows of text, on a canvas of 128 x 64 pixels. LCDFonts.swift has its fonts and icons, made from the Go source of the deck. LCDModel.swift makes the screens from the state.
    • Panel.swift: the parts of the panel of the deck: plates, the display, windows, keys, the confirmation. Theme.swift: the day and night palettes of the deck, and the formats.
    • Store.swift: the settings, the last status and the pairing, in the keychain.
    • Fonts/: IBM Plex Mono (SIL Open Font License), the same files as the deck.
  • Phone/: the iPhone app: the main screen (PhoneApp.swift), the files (FilesView.swift), the setup (SetupView.swift), the search for the deck (Finder.swift), the pairing for push messages (Push.swift), and the settings for the watch (WatchLink.swift).
  • Watch/: the watch app.
  • Complication/: the WidgetKit extension with the complications of the watch.
  • Widget/: the WidgetKit extension with the widgets of the iPhone. Shared/DeckWidget.swift has the timeline and the views that both have.
  • Notifications/: the notification service extension that decrypts the push messages.
  • Push/PushCrypto.swift: the key agreement and the decryption of the push messages.
  • Tests/: the tests of PushCrypto, with vectors from the Go code of the deck.
  • icon/render.swift: draws the app icon, a blue dot-matrix display with "PD". Run swift icon/render.swift <folder> and copy AppIcon-phone.png and AppIcon-watch.png to Phone/Assets.xcassets/AppIcon.appiconset/AppIcon.png and Watch/Assets.xcassets/AppIcon.appiconset/AppIcon.png.
  • project.yml: the XcodeGen file of Printdeck.xcodeproj. After a change to project.yml, run xcodegen in this folder.

The deployment targets are iOS 17 and watchOS 11. The project was built and tested with Xcode 27.

Install

  1. Set DEVELOPMENT_TEAM in project.yml to your team ID and run xcodegen. The project uses automatic signing.
  2. The bundle IDs start with BUNDLE_PREFIX (org.beetlebug.printdeck): the iPhone app, then .notifications, .widget, .watchkitapp and .watchkitapp.widget. The keychain group is the team ID, the prefix and .shared. If your team cannot use this prefix, change BUNDLE_PREFIX in project.yml and run xcodegen.
  3. Open Printdeck.xcodeproj, connect the iPhone, choose the Printdeck scheme and the iPhone, and run. The watch app is in the iPhone app.
  4. For the watch, choose the PrintdeckWatch scheme and the watch, and run. Xcode adds the watch to the team and its profiles. The watch needs Developer Mode, which shows on the watch after Xcode pairs with it. The watch and the Mac must be on the same network. If Xcode does not find the watch, turn off Bluetooth on the iPhone for a minute.
  5. Open Printdeck on the iPhone. It finds the deck on the network. Tap Connect, then tap Allow on the touch screen of the deck. The app gets its own key from the deck and sends the address and the key to the watch.
  6. To add the complication, edit the watch face and choose Printdeck.

When the deck is not in the list, open Menu > By hand. Type the address and tap Ask the deck for a key, or type the key from http://printdeck.local/system and tap Save.

Push messages

The deck sends the messages through the relay (relay/README.md). The relay and Apple see the encrypted message only.

  1. In Menu > Notifications on the iPhone, tap Pair for notifications. iOS asks to allow notifications, and gives the app a device token.
  2. The app posts the token to the relay (POST /register). The relay sends a code in a background push. The app posts the token with the code, and gets a send token.
  3. The app makes an X25519 key pair and pairs with the deck (POST /api/plugin/printdeck/push/pair) with its public key, the device token, the send token and the relay URL. The reply has the public key of the deck. Both make the message key with HKDF-SHA256 (the salt is the device token, the info is printdeck push v1).
  4. The message key goes into the keychain group, for the notification service extension. The deck sends "This phone gets the messages of your prints."
  5. The extension decrypts the key e of each push (AES-256-GCM) and shows its title and body. When it cannot, iOS shows the alert of the push, "Printdeck: A message from your printer".

The app pairs again when iOS gives it a new device token. Unpair removes the phone from the deck; Settings > Phones on the deck has the same list. A build that Xcode installs uses the sandbox of APNs, and a TestFlight or App Store build uses production. The app reads the environment from its provisioning profile. PUSH_RELAY in project.yml is the URL of the relay.

To test: install from Xcode, pair, and start a print. "First layer done" comes after the first layer. The tests of PushCrypto run with the Printdeck scheme (Product > Test).

Network

The deck serves plain HTTP on the local network. NSAllowsLocalNetworking in the Info.plist of each target allows it. The deck announces itself with Bonjour as _octoprint._tcp. The iPhone app finds it and saves its IPv4 address, because watchOS may not resolve printdeck.local. The watch reaches the deck on Wi-Fi, or through the iPhone when it is near. iOS asks once for the permission to use the local network.

The address can have a port, as 192.168.1.50:8080. An address with no scheme gets http://.

API

The App Keys flow of OctoPrint gives the app its key. It needs no key:

  • POST /plugin/appkeys/request with {"app": "Printdeck on iPhone"}: the touch screen of the deck asks to allow the app, and the reply has a token.
  • GET /plugin/appkeys/request/<token>: 202 while the deck waits, 200 with api_key after Allow, and 404 after Deny or after 2 minutes.

All other requests have the header X-Api-Key.

  • GET /api/job: the state text, the file name and the time of the print.
  • GET /api/printer: the temperatures. The deck sends 409 when the printer is not connected, and the app shows no temperatures then.
  • GET /api/plugin/printdeck: the roll in the printer, the print with its layer, progress and seconds left, and the camera (its name, state and size, or null with no camera).
  • GET /webcam/snapshot?w=..: the newest picture of the camera, as a JPEG w pixels wide, for the watch.
  • GET /webcam/stream?w=..&fps=..: the camera as an MJPEG stream, for the iPhone. URLSession gives each part of the stream as a response of its own.
  • POST /api/job: {"command":"pause","action":"pause"}, {"command":"pause","action":"resume"} and {"command":"cancel"}.
  • GET /api/files?recursive=true: the files. POST /api/files/local/<path> with {"command":"select","print":true} prints one. The deck asks on its touch screen when the material of the file is not the material in the printer.
  • GET /api/plugin/printdeck/model.png?w=..&h=..&mono=1: the model of the print in 1-bit dots, for the model page of the watch. The page shows when the deck answers.
  • POST /api/plugin/printdeck/push/pair, DELETE /api/plugin/printdeck/push/<id>: the pairing for push messages.

Screenshots in the simulator

In a debug build, the environment variables PRINTDECK_ADDRESS and PRINTDECK_KEY set the deck, and PRINTDECK_SCREEN opens a screen: menu or cancel on the watch, files, setup, camera or loading on the iPhone. With xcrun simctl launch, give them the prefix SIMCTL_CHILD_. The simulated deck of printdeck app -sim reads its API key from OCTOPRINT_API_KEY.
